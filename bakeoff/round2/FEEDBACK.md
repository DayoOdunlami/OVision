# What round 1 taught us

Round 1 asked three studios to make this the app a family would choose every day. The
three entries were **Table**, **Tend** and **Quiet Waters**. Quiet Waters won, and this
branch is built on it. Every entry is still on its branch (`bakeoff/r1-a` Table,
`bakeoff/r1-b` Tend, `bakeoff/r1-c` Quiet Waters), including its reviews of the others.
You may read and borrow from all of them.

This file is the client's own feedback, from using all three, followed by where the three
studios' reviews of each other agreed. It is part of the round 2 brief.

## The client, on each entry

**Table** felt basic, "a little bit like a form".
- It rebuilt the koi as flat cut-out fish that are worse than the existing ones.
- The puzzle words stick rather than fall with weight.
- The prayer needs a tap for every line.
- The season page is text-heavy.
- **Credit:** its front door. Names first, tap who's here, and off you go.

**Tend** felt like one product. The type, icons and colour make it a brand, and it is
calm and still.
- Its garden illustration is genuinely lovely, but static. The fish in it look like dead
  fish floating on top.
- Every room is the same pattern: a beautiful picture at the top, then a well-made list
  or form underneath. Adding the season's words doesn't feel like anything worthwhile
  happened.
- **Credit:** the history of who prayed when, filterable by person, is a strong
  foundation, and its play options are good.

**Quiet Waters** felt like a real upgrade of what existed.
- It kept the koi, the physics and the Amen moment, and it ties prayer, play and
  confession into one place.
- Play is the best of the three.
- *Wandered?* is a genuinely new idea.
- *Into the deep* (dropping a stone into the pond) suits the client better than the old
  stone field. The children may miss pulling weeds and moving stones.
- **Against it:** it's busy, with lily pads, fish and text competing. Scroll bars appear
  and break the calm. The prayer has a timer, though you can skip it.

## The client, on what is still missing

This is the reason round 2 exists.

> All of them struggled with the family vision board. Tend and Quiet Waters both had a
> half-decent season, but it felt like a list that's done well. A lovely image and text.
> Once I've done it, I'm not sure why next week I'd want to look at it unless I want to
> update it. It didn't give me something I really want to glance at, daily or weekly.

> The original, while the implementation wasn't amazing, had visual interaction
> reinforcing what the words meant: the fish behaved differently near different words.
> It was something you wanted to look at.

> A season's words become like a diary entry you check each term. A vision board is
> something you leave on the side so it helps you remember what your purpose is. You look
> at it each day, but not because you want to change it each day. It should help you know
> what to say yes to and what to say no to.

The client also misses the other worlds:
- the vine that writes a word as it grows (Flourish and Abide);
- the starlings that write words in the sky (Murmuration).

He wonders whether the answer is choosing your world: the same practice in a pond, a
garden or a sky, each playing to its own strengths. He is not attached to vines in
particular. If there is a better way to embody growth and nature, he wants to see it.

**One more lesson.** Agents struggle to make motion lifelike without a reference. The koi
took many iterations, and only worked once the client found code where someone had
already solved fish movement. Reuse proven motion, or find a reference before you invent
it.

## Where the studios' reviews agreed

All three studios ranked Quiet Waters first, including the two that didn't build it. They
agreed on these points:

- **Keep the living koi.** Table's flat fish and Tend's static fish were both called a
  step down.
- **Don't lock play behind prayer.**
- **On a phone, Quiet Waters' fish cover words.** A koi hides the line explaining how to
  feed them.
- **Tap targets in the puzzles fall below 48 px** for short words like "I".
- **Tend reads like a premium brand's website:** hero headline, cards, a long scroll.
  It's beautiful, but it's a place you browse more than a place you pray.
- **Tend's adult prayer writing and adult confession are the best of the three.**
- **The best season ritual is Quiet Waters' "pass the tablet":** one word each, feeding
  the family's word.
