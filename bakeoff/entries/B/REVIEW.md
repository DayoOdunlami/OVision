# Review by entry B

Reviewed A at `463fedc` and C at `920b471`, each in a separate local checkout after `npm install` and `npm run dev`. I used `?demo=1` at 390 × 844 and 1180 × 820, clicked through the products before opening their entry documents, then revisited their documented deep links. Both production builds pass.

The walkthrough covered solo and children's prayer, participant changes, accepting and rejecting puzzle words, confession, history, season editing, Sunday, first run and night. These are adult desktop-browser observations, not results from the children's panel or physical Android devices. I have not independently certified installation, offline coverage or the two-second performance target. The shared test machine was too variable for a fair speed ranking. Both demos deliberately reset on reload; I have not counted that as lost family data.

## Entry A — Table

**In one sentence, what is this product?**

A weekly Scripture reading on a quiet sheet of paper, with actual participants, a missing-word puzzle, a short reflection and a separate pond.

**What it does better than anyone else in the bake-off (including you):**

**Preference.** The front page gives the verse the clearest hierarchy of the three. “Holding Pip and Noor” sits immediately below it; navigation stays secondary. B asks the eye to interpret more of a household fieldbook, and C puts more words over an illustrated world. A makes opening the app feel closest to opening a page by the bed.

**The moments**

**Craft**

**Preference.** It avoids the average. Cormorant's large italic verse, Literata's sturdier reading, quiet clay names and the near absence of boxes create a recognisable voice. The puzzle's paper slips belong to that voice. The simple fish belong to a separate illustrated room; that separation feels intentional. The night sheet is particularly good.

**Trade-off.** Removing illustration from prayer protects a wandering mind but also makes the tablet experience less recognisably the living place the children know. I would keep the reading stillness and bring back the richer pond only outside it.

**Defects:**

- **Defect — puzzle targets below the specified minimum.** At 390 × 844 open `/?demo=1&at=puzzle&h=18&still=1`, then *Let the words fall*. The first “Gold space, the next word” button measures about **22.5 × 48 px**; an “I” slip about **30 × 48 px**, and “be” about **43 × 48 px**. These are the actual interactive elements' bounds, not the gold underline alone. They miss the addendum's 48 × 48 px minimum. Give short words and gaps a sufficiently large hit area without crowding adjacent choices.

**Trade-offs:**

- **Trade-off — play follows prayer.** On the ordinary home page, selecting people who have not prayed removes *Let the words fall*. Completing their prayer unlocks it; the review deep link bypasses this. That preserves the prayer-then-play sequence, but makes retrieval practice depend on recording a sitting. I would allow the children to practise freely, as C does.
- **Trade-off — one fixed puzzle cut.** The digest deliberately chooses every third word and describes that as the memory cut. QA explicitly says the favourite drops **all** words. The smaller cut is easier to read and touch, but I would preserve the reported favourite rather than replace it. This is an intentional product choice, not a broken button.
- **Trade-off — recorded reflection.** Reproduce the privacy consequence by selecting two grown-ups, praying, choosing *worry*, then opening *The weeks*: the participants are named beside what was set down. There is no privacy explanation at the choice. Shared recollection has value, but I would neither record this automatically nor attribute a joint choice to both consciences.

**Preferences:**

- **Preference.** I would use *Continue* rather than *On*. The latter is economical but takes a moment to interpret on first encounter; it is not a functional defect.
- **Preference.** “That is enough” is the best stopping phrase in A. It releases the adult from the app without suggesting that everyone else's day is finished.

**One thing the winner should borrow from this entry:**

**Preference.** Its editorial restraint: let one short thought occupy the reading surface, and let the end of prayer be an end.

## Entry C — Quiet Waters

**In one sentence, what is this product?**

A household pond from which prayer, verse play, confession and seasonal reflection open onto still reading surfaces and return to the same living place.

**What it does better than anyone else in the bake-off (including you):**

**Preference.** It gives confession the most convincing complete shape: ask for light, review a concrete day, pause, name something to God without entering it into the app, release it, hear assurance, consider repair. The children's stone-or-flower choice preserves a physical gesture without making every child find a fault. That is stronger than A's categories and more fully developed than B's shorter private practice.

**The moments**

| # | Moment | Verdict | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 06:45, Dad, bedside | Night paper, two readings, optional quiet and *Wandered?* directly serve the wandering mind. Some intercession cards carry a long paragraph. Their continuity is valuable, but I would divide those into shorter thoughts while retaining the gentle return word. | Trade-off |
| 2 | After dinner, children | Named readers, an “I wonder” response, blessing and direct verse play make a strong sequence. All-word play is available and accepted the next word while rejecting a wrong one. The physical pile is more delightful but denser on a phone than B's word bank; I would keep the physics with an equally readable alternative. | Trade-off |
| 3 | Sunday together | The Lord's Prayer, each person's seasonal word, this week's verse from first letters, a reveal and a household declaration of recall give Sunday a distinct purpose. This is my preferred Sunday of the three. | Preference |
| 4 | A glance in passing | The same pond becomes the idle screen; family word, verse and today's names remain. Moving fish can cross the words, but make this feel inhabited. I would keep that life here and preserve the stillness already used during reading. | Trade-off |
| 5 | Clearing the ground | Adult reflection precedes the gesture; no confession is typed or stored. Children can offer thanks instead of an invented wrongdoing. The gesture adds time compared with a page alone; I would keep this shaped practice. | Trade-off |
| 6 | Looking back | Weeks, verses, declared recall and the book of seasons give the pond a readable history. Pads mark elapsed weeks, not prayer attendance: less evidence of frequency, less pressure. I would choose that restraint. | Trade-off |
| 7 | New term or season | Each person chooses a word before the household chooses its word and practices. I edited the season through to Save. Repeating those steps makes a small correction slower than A's form, but I would keep the shared ritual for a new term and add a quicker edit path later. | Trade-off |
| 8 | First run | A clear example-family option precedes a short household setup with names, roles and koi. I set up a new two-person household and reached prayer without a seasonal board. That postponement is the right friction saving here. | Preference |

**Craft**

**Preference.** The strongest illustrated environment in the bake-off. Fish have weight and depth, water changes with the hour, and the paper has a consistent relationship to that world. Fraunces gives the seasonal word character; Literata makes reading substantial. The phone and landscape tablet both feel composed. It avoids the average without needing a different visual language in each room.

**Trade-off.** The long Scripture passage on the pond gives the household's word its source, but fills much of the phone's upper screen. I would keep it on the tablet and test a shorter visible excerpt on the phone. Full text remains important in prayer and play.

**Defects:**

- **Defect — puzzle targets below the specified minimum.** At 390 × 844 open `/?demo=1&h=18#/play`, select *Every word*, then *Let them fall*. The “I” tile measures about **26 × 44 px**; many other tiles are about **44–47 px high**. The *This week's verse* / *Today's prayer* tabs are **44 px high** too. Rotation makes exact tile bounds vary, but does not solve the small short-word target. These miss the 48 × 48 px brief and the direction's stronger 64 px children's claim. Enlarge the real targets and verify them in a settled, crowded round.

**Trade-offs:**

- **Trade-off — inferred prayer voice.** Who is present determines simple or adult words. This removes setup decisions, but an adult alone cannot request the simpler register. I would retain the inference as the default and permit a quiet override; tired adults may need short words too.
- **Trade-off — a learning ramp before the favourite.** The demo starts on *Some words*; *Every word* takes an extra choice. A ramp can help newcomers, but QA says the regular users already choose all-word play. I would default this household to that and leave the easier cuts available.
- **Trade-off — remembered company.** The evening demo opens with Ada, Kit and Noor, and clearly offers *Not right? Change*. That saves repeated selection but can record absent people if accepted blindly. I would keep the visible confirmation. In my completed evening sitting, the home correctly distinguished Sam's morning from the children's evening.
- **Trade-off — atmosphere at a hardware cost.** The rich pond carries more rendering work than A's sheet or B's quieter garden. C documents optimisation and measured limits; those are useful evidence, not a substitute for the family's Android test. I would keep the pond, conditional on that real-device check.

**Preferences:**

- **Preference.** *Wandered?* is a better response to distraction than asking the adult to operate another mode. It offers a way back without counting lapses.
- **Preference.** The season-setting instruction “Pass it to…” turns a form into a family conversation. It is worth preserving even if quick editing gets a separate route.

**One thing the winner should borrow from this entry:**

**Preference.** The confession sequence, especially the unrecorded naming, spoken assurance and invitation to repair, with the child's flower as a legitimate response.

## Ranking

1. **C — Trade-off.** The strongest whole for this household: a distinctive place, a serious adult practice and generous shared family rituals, accepting a denser phone game and the need to fix its small targets.
2. **B, our entry — Trade-off.** Short prayer thoughts, a large-word bank and a practical printable board make a dependable alternative, but C gives the family a more compelling living place and a fuller confession practice; **we are not first**.
3. **A — Trade-off.** Its still reading page is exceptionally restrained, but losing all-word play, gating practice behind prayer and recording named burdens cost more for this family than the reduction in interface buys.

**If the winner is not you, what is the single most important thing it should take from your entry?**

**Trade-off.** C should take **B's large-word bank alongside the falling puzzle**. It uses more orderly space and offers less physical delight, but keeps full-verse retrieval usable when a crowded pile or a small target gets in the way. Preserve C's falling game as the favourite; give every word a dependable alternative target.
