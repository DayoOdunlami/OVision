# Entry A: Table

One page for the household, not for engineers.

**In one sentence:** One NIV verse for the week, prayed by whoever is actually at the table, then learned by letting the words fall back into place.

**Who it serves, and how:**

- Dad, at 06:45, on the phone: the verse is already there. If he has not prayed, the morning opens on "Be still." He prays in silence, one line at a time, and may set down whatever got in the way. Amen does not tick the children off. He can stop.
- The children, after dinner, on the tablet: the same verse, the same words. They say who is here, pray it aloud, and may set down one kind stone. Then the loose words fall, and they put them back until the verse is whole.
- The household, on Sunday: the week closes with everyone held in that same verse. Monday morning brings a new one, as a small surprise.
- Another family, later: a first run asks who prays here and one word for the season. Nothing in the app assumes this household's names. The water can sit open on a sideboard, with the family word over it, and the fish can be fed.

**Kept, merged, cut, added:**

Kept: one verse all week, the memory puzzle with words that fall, and koi you can feed. Merged: Dad's morning and the children's evening are two sittings of one prayer, recorded per person. Cut: a separate prayer on Sunday, names inside the memorised line, scores, streaks, the vineyard, the stone-field of vices, labs, and sound. Added: a silent morning, a single question for an adult, one kindly stone for a child, a week you can look back over, and a season page where a family word and a word each can be set.

**Why a family would choose it every day:** The phone in the dark shows the verse and nothing else moving. The tablet after dinner does the part the children already love, with the church's wording, unchanged from the prayer. Nobody is told they missed a day. A gap is just an empty mark.

**How it grows:** The sittings, the week, and the season word are one household record on the device. A later round can sync that record, add a sign-in, and let another household start from the same first run. Sound, if it comes, is a bell at the threshold and a human voice for a child who cannot read yet. Not a soundtrack. Selling the NIV text to other families is a licence question for later. This round stays inside Biblica's general-use limits, and the notice is on the screen.
