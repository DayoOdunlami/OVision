When both happen on the same day — an adult praying Scripture for the family at the bedside, and the children after dinner — should the evening be a second sitting of that same passage and those same people, then the verse play, or a different prayer? The app today has one "prayed today" state, so a morning Amen can finish the day before the children arrive; one shared liturgy needs a morning close and an evening close that do not cancel each other; two prayers means two tracks and a different way in.
2. At the kitchen tablet, who has to be able to carry the prayer and the verse play: someone who can read it aloud (an adult or an older child), or a child who cannot read well yet, on their own? With a reader in the room the path is large type, a short register, and a puzzle a child can tap; without one, the same path needs a voice or pictures that carry the words, which is a different product.
3. Should the week's Scripture, the day's pairs, and the verse the children are learning change on Sunday — the day the whole household is on one screen — or on Monday morning? This decides which prayer that shared screen uses, and the day "this week's verse" flips for the children learning it.
4. May the prayers leave the NIV wording in the earliest passages and become one original modern-English voice, quoting a public-domain translation only where a verse is given in full? Keeping that wording preserves lines already being prayed and leaves a licence in the way of any other family; rewriting it changes lines someone may be learning by heart, and every puzzle cut from them.
