# Gate 0 questions: entry C

1. **Whose prayer is 06:45?** Today Dad's morning uses the same rota pair as everyone else, so some days he prays "Florence and me", while his board's commitment is "Ephesians 1:18 over [his wife] and the kids each morning". Should Dad's morning be the family rota pair, a prayer over the whole household, or his own choice? *Why it matters:* it decides whether moments 1 and 2 are one shared daily prayer on two devices or two different practices, and that sets the app's whole structure.
2. **Is the board the family's or each person's?** The brief says "who this family is becoming", but the live board is one adult's personal board ("Dayo · Spring 2026": HYROX, craft, a body goal). The code also has an unused family board and a draft board for each member. For moments 4 and 7, should there be one household board, one per person (children included), or a household board with personal ones behind it? *Why it matters:* it decides what the idle kitchen tablet shows, who answers the questions at the start of a term, and whether children ever make a board.
3. **Must the family's existing data carry over?** On the family's own devices, `familyPrayer`, `flourish.vineyard` and the wall store indices into the hard-coded six-person roster. If an entry becomes the foundation, does it have to keep that history (the prayed days, the log, the vineyard), or can we start a clean, household-agnostic data model with a one-off import, or none at all? *Why it matters:* keeping strict compatibility pins us to the old shapes and the six-person rota. Being free to break them is what makes "another family can set it up" cheap.
4. **Which devices and browsers, exactly?** For example: iPhone with Safari, iPad or Android tablet, and is Sunday on that tablet or on a TV? *Why it matters:* installing to the home screen, keeping the screen awake for the idle tablet, offline behaviour and audio all differ sharply between iOS Safari and Android Chrome. We'd build and test for your real devices first.
5. **Is sound welcome, and is a synthetic voice acceptable?** For a child who can't read yet, the browser's built-in text-to-speech could read the verse and the puzzle words aloud. Would you accept a synthetic voice, or would you rather have silence, a bell only, or recordings you make yourselves? Is any sound acceptable at 06:45 beside a sleeping spouse? *Why it matters:* it decides whether non-readers are served by audio or only by pictures and icons, and whether sound is part of the look at all.
6. **What exactly doesn't land about the stones for you as an adult?** Is it the physical effort and fiddliness, the sins being pre-named rather than your own, doing it as a game beside the children, or that nothing is actually said to God? *Why it matters:* each answer leads to a different moment 5. It could become a quieter adult mode of the same field, a short spoken or written examen with the field kept for the children, or a step that comes after confession rather than replacing it.
7. **Apart from the puzzle and clearing stones, what would the children miss if it disappeared?** For example: their own koi and its colour, the koi rising at Amen, the vineyard, the wall, the starlings, or the prayer changing each week. *Why it matters:* we expect to cut and merge heavily, and we'd rather not cut something a child is attached to that the docs don't mention.
8. **Which wording do the children learn by heart elsewhere (church, school)?** The first four prayers use NIV wording. The rest are original renderings, and the brief allows the World English Bible or King James. *Why it matters:* if the puzzle teaches a different wording from the one they hear on Sundays, learning by heart works against itself. The answer decides whether we keep the NIV-shaped verses for memory work or move everything to one public-domain text.
